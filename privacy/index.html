Privacy policy: the HS&E Test Practice app

This policy is about the HS&E Test Practice iPhone app. Last updated 25 November 2026.

Who we are. The app is made by Andrew Domakin, a sole trader in the UK. Contact: hsande@protonmail.com, 68 Woodland Avenue, Hutton, Brentwood, Essex CM13 1HH.

In short: your answers, scores and settings stay on your iPhone. From version 1.1.3, the app includes a small piece of code from Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram). It tells Meta when the app is installed and opened, so we can see whether our adverts on Facebook and Instagram work. Meta does not get your name, your answers or your scores from this app.

  • No account. There is no sign-up and no log-in. The app never asks your name or email.
  • No server of ours. The app has no server of ours to talk to. The questions are inside the app, and it works with no signal. The only thing the app sends is the install and app-open messages to Meta, described below.
  • Your progress stays on your iPhone. Your answers, scores, test choice, help language and settings are saved only on your iPhone. They are never sent to us or anyone else. Deleting the app deletes them.
  • Meta's code. The app contains Meta's software development kit (SDK), set up for "app events" only. When you install and open the app, it sends Meta: that the app was installed or opened, and when; a random number made on your iPhone for this copy of the app (not your name, phone number or Apple Account); basic facts about the phone and app, such as the model, iOS version, app version, language, time zone, screen size, number of processor cores and mobile network; and, if Meta's code itself has an error or crashes, a report about that. Like any internet connection, it also gives Meta your phone's IP address, from which a rough location can be worked out. It does not send your answers, scores, test choice, help language, searches or purchases. We have turned off Meta's options to send screen names, screen layouts, purchases and typed details. Meta uses this to count installs that came from our adverts and to improve how our adverts are delivered. Meta handles it under its own Privacy Policy (facebook.com/privacy/policy), which also explains your choices about how Meta uses information from other apps.
  • No tracking prompt, no advertising identifier. The app never asks to track you and does not collect your iPhone's advertising identifier. Because you have not allowed tracking, iOS blocks Meta's tracking address. Apple tells Meta which adverts led to installs through Apple's own SKAdNetwork, which does not identify you.
  • No adverts in the app. The app shows no adverts. Meta's code is there only to measure our own adverts on Facebook and Instagram.
  • Read aloud uses your iPhone's own voices, on the phone. Nothing you hear is sent anywhere.

Buying full access. The one-off purchase is made through Apple's App Store. Apple handles the payment under its own privacy policy (apple.com/legal/privacy). We never see your name, card or Apple Account details. Apple sends us sales reports with totals, such as how many purchases were made in each country, which do not identify you. "Restore purchase" asks Apple whether your Apple Account has already bought full access; we see nothing of this.

Why we do this. We rely on our legitimate interest in knowing whether our adverts work, so we can keep the app's price low. We keep the data sent to Meta to the minimum Meta needs.

How long it is kept. We do not receive or store this data ourselves. Meta keeps it under its Privacy Policy. Deleting the app deletes the random number from your iPhone; a new one is made if you install it again.

Links out of the app. The app's About screen links to this privacy policy, to the App Store to rate the app, and to the Open Government Licence on the National Archives website. They open under each website's own privacy policy.

Our website and email sign-up. This website is separate from the app. If you gave us your email address on it to hear when the app is out, that is covered by How we use your details.

Children. The app is meant for people preparing for the CITB HS&E test, usually 16 and over. It is not meant for children under 13. The only data it sends is the install and app-open messages to Meta described above.

If you email us. If you write to hsande@protonmail.com, we use your email and message only to reply to you, and delete them within 12 months of our last reply.

Your rights. We hold no app data about you: the install and app-open messages go to Meta, not to us. To stop them, delete the app. To ask Meta about its data, use the contact routes in Meta's Privacy Policy. If you have a question or complaint about privacy, write to hsande@protonmail.com. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office: ico.org.uk.

Changes. If the app starts to collect more data, we will update this page before that version is released, and change the date above.
